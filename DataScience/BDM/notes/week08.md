## 1. Case Study Overview: XYZ Limited

This week focuses on the practical application of Financial Ratio Analysis and Financial Statement Analysis using a comprehensive, real-world case study of **XYZ Limited**, a leading manufacturer of commercial vehicles [1061].

Ratios standardize the raw numbers into meaningful metrics that allow business data managers, analysts, and investors to make informed decisions [1061].

## 3. Step-by-Step Analysis of the Case Study Questions

Below is the detailed, step-by-step mathematical derivation for each of the twelve analytical questions associated with the XYZ Limited case study.

### Question 1: Net Profit Margin
**Question:** What is the Net Profit Margin for XYZ Limited in FY 2025 [1063]?
*   **A.** 8.44%
*   **B.** 11.25%
*   **C.** 13.75%
*   **D.** 6.50%

#### Mathematical Derivation:
The Net Profit Margin measures the percentage of revenue remaining after all operating and non-operating expenses, interest, and taxes have been deducted.
$$\text{Net Profit Margin} = \frac{\text{Net Profit (PAT)}}{\text{Revenue from Operations}} \times 100$$
$$\text{Net Profit Margin} = \frac{3,375}{40,000} \times 100$$
$$\text{Net Profit Margin} = 0.084375 \times 100 = 8.4375\% \approx 8.44\%$$

*   **Correct Option:** **A. 8.44%** [1063]

### Question 2: Return on Assets (ROA)
**Question:** Calculate the Return on Assets (ROA) for the year [1063].
*   **A.** 8.44%
*   **B.** 27.00%
*   **C.** 11.25%
*   **D.** 15.00%

#### Mathematical Derivation:
Return on Assets measures how efficiently a company uses its assets to generate net profits.
$$\text{ROA} = \frac{\text{Net Profit (PAT)}}{\text{Total Assets}} \times 100$$
$$\text{ROA} = \frac{3,375}{30,000} \times 100$$
$$\text{ROA} = 0.1125 \times 100 = 11.25\%$$

*   **Correct Option:** **C. 11.25%** [1063]

### Question 3: Return on Equity (ROE)
**Question:** What is the Return on Equity (ROE) generated by the company [1063]?
*   **A.** 11.25%
*   **B.** 27.00%
*   **C.** 30.50%
*   **D.** 22.00%

#### Mathematical Derivation:
Return on Equity measures the profitability of a business relative to the book value of shareholder's equity. It reveals how much profit a company generates with the money shareholders have invested.
$$\text{ROE} = \frac{\text{Net Profit (PAT)}}{\text{Total Shareholder's Equity}} \times 100$$
$$\text{ROE} = \frac{3,375}{12,500} \times 100$$
$$\text{ROE} = 0.2700 \times 100 = 27.00\%$$

*   **Correct Option:** **B. 27.00%** [1063]

### Question 4: Debtors Turnover Ratio
**Question:** Assuming average debtors are equal to the year-end debtors, what is the Debtors Turnover Ratio [1063]?
*   **A.** 8.00 times
*   **B.** 10.00 times
*   **C.** 12.00 times
*   **D.** 14.00 times

#### Mathematical Derivation:
The Debtors Turnover Ratio measures how quickly a company collects cash from its credit customers.
$$\text{Debtors Turnover Ratio} = \frac{\text{Revenue from Operations}}{\text{Average Debtors}}$$
Given that Average Debtors = Year-End Debtors = 4,000 Crores [1062, 1063]:
$$\text{Debtors Turnover Ratio} = \frac{40,000}{4,000} = 10.00 \text{ times}$$

*   **Correct Option:** **B. 10.00 times** [1063]

### Question 5: Dividend Yield
**Question:** Calculate the Dividend Yield based on the current market price [1064].
*   **A.** 1.33%
*   **B.** 2.50%
*   **C.** 3.47%
*   **D.** 4.00%

#### Mathematical Derivation:
Dividend Yield measures the cash return an investor gets per share relative to the stock's current market price.
$$\text{Dividend Yield} = \frac{\text{Dividend Declared per Share}}{\text{Current Market Price per Share}} \times 100$$
Given: Dividend per Share = ₹2.00 and Market Price = ₹150.00 [1062]:
$$\text{Dividend Yield} = \frac{2.00}{150.00} \times 100$$
$$\text{Dividend Yield} = 0.013333 \times 100 = 1.333\% \approx 1.33\%$$

*   **Correct Option:** **A. 1.33%** [1064]

### Question 6: Asset Turnover Ratio
**Question:** Calculate the Asset Turnover Ratio [1064].
*   **A.** 1.50 times
*   **B.** 1.33 times
*   **C.** 2.00 times
*   **D.** 0.85 times

#### Mathematical Derivation:
The Asset Turnover Ratio measures the efficiency with which a company uses its assets to generate revenue.
$$\text{Asset Turnover Ratio} = \frac{\text{Revenue from Operations}}{\text{Total Assets}}$$
$$\text{Asset Turnover Ratio} = \frac{40,000}{30,000} = 1.3333 \approx 1.33 \text{ times}$$

*   **Correct Option:** **B. 1.33 times** [1064]

### Question 7: Inventory Turnover Ratio
**Question:** Based on the Cost of Materials Consumed, what is the Inventory Turnover Ratio [1064]?
*   **A.** 11.42 times
*   **B.** 10.00 times
*   **C.** 8.00 times
*   **D.** 6.50 times

#### Mathematical Derivation:
The Inventory Turnover Ratio measures how many times a company's inventory is sold and replaced over a period. In manufacturing firms, the Cost of Materials Consumed is often used as the numerator in the absence of a stated Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) [1061, 1064].
$$\text{Inventory Turnover Ratio} = \frac{\text{Cost of Materials Consumed}}{\text{Inventory}}$$
Given: Cost of Materials Consumed = 28,000 Crores and Inventory = 3,500 Crores [1061, 1062]:
$$\text{Inventory Turnover Ratio} = \frac{28,000}{3,500} = 8.00 \text{ times}$$

*   **Correct Option:** **C. 8.00 times** [1064]

### Question 8: Earnings Per Share (EPS)
**Question:** What is the Earnings Per Share (EPS) for XYZ Limited [1064]?
*   **A.** ₹6.75
*   **B.** ₹9.00
*   **C.** ₹11.25
*   **D.** ₹25.00

#### Mathematical Derivation:
Earnings Per Share indicates the portion of a company's profit allocated to each outstanding share of common stock.
$$\text{EPS} = \frac{\text{Net Profit / Profit After Tax (PAT)}}{\text{Number of Outstanding Shares}}$$
Outstanding Shares = 500 Crores (since Face Value is ₹1 and Equity Share Capital is ₹500 Crores) [1062]:
$$\text{EPS} = \frac{\text{₹3,375 Crores}}{\text{500 Crores Shares}} = \text{₹}6.75$$

*   **Correct Option:** **A. ₹6.75** [1064]

### Question 9: Price to Earnings (PE) Ratio
**Question:** Using the EPS calculated in the previous question, what is the Price to Earnings (PE) Ratio [1064]?
*   **A.** 18.50
*   **B.** 20.00
*   **C.** 22.22
*   **D.** 25.40

#### Mathematical Derivation:
The Price-to-Earnings Ratio relates a company's share price to its earnings per share, indicating how much investors are willing to pay per rupee of earnings [539, 1070].
$$\text{PE Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Market Price per Share}}{\text{Earnings Per Share (EPS)}}$$
Given: Current Market Price = ₹150.00 and EPS = ₹6.75:
$$\text{PE Ratio} = \frac{150.00}{6.75} = 22.2222 \approx 22.22$$

*   **Correct Option:** **C. 22.22** [535]

### Question 10: Debt-Equity Ratio
**Question:** Assess the solvency of the company by calculating the Debt-Equity Ratio [1065].
*   **A.** 0.12
*   **B.** 0.20
*   **C.** 0.25
*   **D.** 0.50

#### Mathematical Derivation:
The Debt-Equity Ratio is a solvency metric that measures the proportion of debt used to finance the company's assets relative to the value of shareholder's equity.
$$\text{Debt-Equity Ratio} = \frac{\text{Total Debt (Borrowings)}}{\text{Total Shareholder's Equity}}$$
Given: Total Debt = 2,500 Crores and Total Shareholder's Equity = 12,500 Crores [1062]:
$$\text{Debt-Equity Ratio} = \frac{2,500}{12,500} = 0.20$$

*   **Correct Option:** **B. 0.20** [1065]

### Question 11: EBITDA Calculation
**Question:** Based on the Profit & Loss statement, what is the EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization) for XYZ Limited [1065]?
*   **A.** ₹4,500 Crores
*   **B.** ₹5,500 Crores
*   **C.** ₹6,300 Crores
*   **D.** ₹34,500 Crores

#### Mathematical Derivation:
EBITDA represents a company's operating profitability before non-operating expenses, interest, taxes, and non-cash accounting charges.
$$\text{EBITDA} = \text{Profit Before Tax (PBT)} + \text{Finance Costs (Interest)} + \text{Depreciation}$$
Given: PBT = 4,500 Crores, Interest = 200 Crores, and Depreciation = 800 Crores [1061]:
$$\text{EBITDA} = 4,500 + 200 + 800 = 5,500 \text{ Crores}$$

*   **Correct Option:** **B. ₹5,500 Crores** [1065]

### Question 12: Total Operating Expenses
**Question:** What are the total Operating Expenses (excluding depreciation and finance costs) for the company [1065]?
*   **A.** ₹35,500 Crores
*   **B.** ₹28,000 Crores
*   **C.** ₹34,500 Crores
*   **D.** ₹30,500 Crores

#### Mathematical Derivation:
Total Operating Expenses represent the day-to-day cash expenses incurred to run the core operations of the manufacturing business. They include material consumption, employee benefits, and other general overheads, but exclude depreciation (non-cash) and interest (finance costs) [1061, 1065].
$$\text{Operating Expenses} = \text{Cost of Materials Consumed} + \text{Employee Benefits Expense} + \text{Other Expenses}$$
Given [1061]:
*   Cost of Materials Consumed: 28,000 Crores
*   Employee Benefits Expense: 2,500 Crores
*   Other Expenses: 4,000 Crores
$$\text{Operating Expenses} = 28,000 + 2,500 + 4,000 = 34,500 \text{ Crores}$$

*   **Correct Option:** **C. ₹34,500 Crores** [1065]

## 4. Key Management Insights from XYZ Limited Case Study

This case study demonstrates several key financial management principles:
1.  **Low Debt Risk:** With a Debt-Equity Ratio of **0.20**, XYZ Limited exhibits strong long-term solvency and low financial risk [1062, 1065]. It has considerable unused debt capacity if it wishes to expand production lines or invest in R&D [536, 1066].
2.  **Asset Efficiency:** An Asset Turnover of **1.33 times** combined with a Net Profit Margin of **8.44%** indicates that the company is utilizing its fixed and current assets effectively to drive top-line sales, avoiding the trap of "lazy" or unproductive asset over-investment [541, 1063, 1064, 1071].
3.  **Healthy Cash Flows:** Because depreciation of ₹800 Crores is a non-cash expense, the actual cash generated from operations is higher than the accounting Net Profit (PAT) of ₹3,375 Crores, maintaining a strong liquidity position to pay short-term bills [537, 538, 1061, 1067, 1068].
